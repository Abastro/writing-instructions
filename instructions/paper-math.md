# Mathematical Paper Writing

## Parameter

- **Intended reader**: Mathematical background, writing expectations.
- **Writing task**:  What to prepare or revise, the format and length.

## Instructions

- Develop the argument from the main idea down to the details.
    State the mathematical goal and give the simplest account of the strategy that the intended reader can follow.
    As you develop each part, show what it contributes to the larger argument before working through its details.
- Let the reader’s next question guide the order within each part.
    If a natural approach reveals an obstacle, show where it fails. Introduce the new idea as soon as the reader can see what problem it solves.
- Connect sentences through their subjects.
    Begin with an object already in the reader’s attention when that helps them follow the new claim.
- Prefer conventional notation and preserve the manuscript’s conventions.
    Name a quantity when the reader needs to track it. Write one-off expressions directly when clearer.
    Introduce related symbols together when their relationship matters.
- Make clear what each displayed equation establishes.
    Draw attention to the term, cancellation, or bound that explains the next step.
- Preserve hypotheses, quantifier order, and parameter dependencies.
    Distinguish intuition from proof and flag any gap the source leaves open.
- Check each major transition from the reader’s position at that point.
    Can the reader see why the next step matters before taking it? In a proof, can they tell which hypothesis or earlier result justifies the step?
